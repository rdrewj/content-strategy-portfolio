Case Study

Scaling self-service at Schwab: Answer Widgets

I built the content inventory, search-term mapping, and QA process that helped scale an existing search feature from a 50-widget pilot to more than 500 answers.

Organization

Charles Schwab

Scope

Client and prospect search experiences

Timeline

2021 launch; reporting through February 2022

Role

Content strategy — structure, taxonomy, QA

Contribution & evidence

What I owned: Content inventory, FAQ selection, organization by experience, search-term mapping, sprint structure, and content QA. The widget interface was already in place when I joined; my work focused on scaling its content.

How outcomes were measured: The original project presentation records delivery milestones and Adobe Analytics reporting through February 2022. Interaction counts indicate use, not confirmed task completion or avoided support calls.

About the visuals: The interface screenshot comes from the original project presentation. The delivery diagram is a portfolio reconstruction. Historical interface copy is shown as project evidence, not current trading guidance.

The problem

Automation stalled. The library needed a human system.

When I joined Schwab in spring of 2021, Answer Widgets, contextual FAQ surfaces embedded in the site's search results page, had been live for less than a year. The pilot had produced 50 widgets. The goal was to scale to 500 by year's end through an automated ingestion process that would pull FAQs from across the ecosystem.

When the planned automation did not materialize, my role shifted from stewarding imported content to assembling the FAQ inventory and preparing it for manual delivery.

The challenge wasn't just volume. It was creating a scalable taxonomy and keyword architecture that would allow the right answer to surface for the right search query, at a moment when users were trying to act, not browse.

The strategy

Build the master list. Get the keywords right.

I started by pulling a full content report from the Drupal team: every page across both the client and prospect experiences. Filtering by accordion component type (the CMS pattern used for FAQs) gave me a rough inventory. That component filter identified candidate FAQs; I then reviewed the inventory and selected relevant content for Answer Widgets. The source component was a starting point for selection, rather than a reason to include every item.

The result was a master list organized by experience area — trade, wire, beneficiary, taxes, and more — that gave the team a single source of truth for the library. Keywords were the next critical layer. Using Adobe Analytics search data, I identified the actual terms users were entering for each topic, then mapped those phrases to the corresponding widgets. Getting this right was the difference between a widget that surfaces and one that doesn't.

My contribution was the content layer behind this experience: selecting FAQs, grouping them by experience, adding keywords based on Adobe Analytics search data, and checking those mappings in QA. The saved presentation documents that method, but does not preserve the individual query-to-widget mappings.

The process

Batched sprints. Rigorous QA. Consistent velocity.

With the master list complete, I designed a delivery workflow: batches of 50 widgets per biweekly sprint, moving from content build to development handoff to QA. My role shifted from inventory and mapping work to quality control at the back end of each sprint, reviewing each new experience to confirm keywords mapped correctly and that no formatting or grammatical errors had been introduced in development.

The batching approach kept the work manageable, and the QA step ensured every widget that went live met the same standard as the first.

Measurement notes and source limits

The presentation's narrative reports 557 widgets, while its chart shows 555 from November through January and 557 in February. A chart note also refers to 550 completed by November. I use “500+” for the launch milestone because the exact November count is inconsistent.

The project summary reports 605K expansions by the end of 2021. The analytics slide uses the label “views” and flags Adobe tracking issues in June and July. These records support reporting engagement, but not a precise causal estimate of the expansion program's impact.

The presentation notes seasonal search spikes in February and March. Reduced support-call volume was an intended benefit; no measured reduction or task-completion result is included.

What came next

Maintenance, partnerships, and continuous improvement

Following launch, the work shifted to stewardship. The full library was audited twice to address issues introduced by a site-wide design system update. New widgets were developed in partnership with business stakeholders to support seasonal moments (tax season) and product launches (Thematic Investing). The longer-term roadmap included re-initiating automation, integrating the Schwab Assistant into search, and adding an in-experience feedback mechanism to surface content gaps from user behavior.
