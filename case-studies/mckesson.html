My contribution: I led the content audit, enhancement framework, editorial standards, stakeholder alignment, and phased rollout. McKesson partnered with content agency MSP-C on evaluation, restructuring, and enhancement; business-unit teams developed and approved content with the corporate team.

The problem

Busy healthcare professionals were reaching dead ends

Visitors needed to understand a solution, compare their options, or reach a sales team. Too often, company-focused language and dense paragraphs made those tasks harder. Some pages lacked useful next steps; others repeated content already available elsewhere. Quality and structure varied across business units.

The audit evaluated content accuracy, uniqueness, and performance, including pageviews, bounce rate, conversion rate, and search keywords. It also checked references to divested business lines. Those findings informed decisions to keep, revise, consolidate, or retire content.

The work in practice

Making Onmark’s offer easier to understand

The Onmark page shows how the enhancement framework changed the customer experience. The original page introduced broad “solutions for community practices.” The revised page named the service, explained its benefits, and made contact and further exploration more prominent.

  1. Name what the customer is looking for. “Onmark Group Purchasing Organization (GPO) Services” replaced the generic heading, bringing the brand and service category together.
  2. Make the benefits easier to scan. Descriptive subheads and a bulleted membership-benefits list replaced dense blocks of company-focused copy.
  3. Make the next step visible. A contact module beside the body copy gave visitors a more prominent path to get in touch. The original page had contact language, but no comparable module above the fold.
  4. Support visitors who need more information. Related articles, solutions, and downloadable resources gave buyers ways to continue evaluating their options.

The shared standard

A writing guide that connected content decisions to customer needs

I led the creation of a digital writing guide that brought McKesson’s brand voice and search requirements into one editorial standard. It connected business goals to writing decisions and showed how different page types should help visitors move through evaluation.

Resolving the tension between readability and search

McKesson’s voice called for clear, confident, caring language. Search required the technical terms customers actually used. The guide’s resolution was specific: keep descriptive keywords in headlines, explain features and benefits in natural language, and shorten sentences or break up paragraphs when technical phrases made copy dense.

Defining what each page needed to do

The guide distinguished between needs pages, which help people explore a group of solutions, and solution pages, which support a more specific purchasing decision. Blog content provides education and links to relevant offerings. This gave writers a purpose for each page and a reason for each related-content module.

The 60-page guide also documented business goals, content metrics, voice principles, and a shared vocabulary. It directed contributors to Jira for content requests, connecting editorial guidance to a defined route for new pages and updates.

Delivery across business units

A common framework with a phased rollout

The work required coordination between corporate digital marketing, MSP-C, and five business units. Business-unit teams supplied expertise and approvals, while the enhancement work used product information and sales materials to make each page more useful. The rollout was staggered by business unit to avoid bottlenecks within both the business units and corporate marketing.

The audit covered the site broadly; the delivery scope combined page enhancements, consolidation, and new navigation content. The final report recorded 142 pages across these four work categories:

About the measurement

The report’s headline result is 82%; summing its January–October lead data produces approximately 83%. The supplied report does not explain the difference, so the headline is retained as the reported result and the chart totals are shown separately.

The original goal was 25% lead growth sitewide. Because that target and the reported result cover different page populations, they are not treated as a direct target-versus-result comparison.

These are observed program results. The report does not isolate the impact of individual changes or provide a measured task-completion outcome.

Reflection

Making the next content decision easier

This project brought page-level writing and enterprise content strategy together. Each improvement answered a specific customer need; the guide translated those decisions into a standard that other contributors could apply. The result was a documented approach to deciding what a page should say, how it should be structured, and where it should take the reader next.
