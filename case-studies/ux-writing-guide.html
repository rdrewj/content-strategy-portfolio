Case Study

A writing guide people could find and use

I moved Schwab’s UX writing guide into its design system and rewrote it as a shared standard. Monthly visits rose from roughly 12 to more than 100, and the guide became the knowledge base for an AI writing agent.

Organization

Charles Schwab

Scope

UX writing standards, Everest design system

Role

Senior manager, UX content strategy

Status

Live in Everest

What I owned: Migration strategy, information architecture, editorial standards, and the publishing process with the design-system team.

The problem

Useful guidance, buried in SharePoint

Schwab’s UX writing standards were scattered across 10–15 SharePoint pages. Years of piecemeal contributions had left conflicting guidance, broken links, and outdated accessibility and disclosure references.

The guide received roughly a dozen visits a month. Finding it usually required a direct link or knowing exactly what to search for.

The guide needed a home in the tools designers and writers already used.

The strategy

Make the guide part of the design system

I chose Everest, Schwab’s design system, as the new home. The guide would define house style and complement Everest’s component-level text guidance. I also planned its structure for use as an AI knowledge base.

The process

Agree on scope, then establish how to publish

Content strategy peers had different views on the guide’s scope and location. I made the case for concise guidance in Everest and worked with them to agree on that direction.

I worked with the design-system team to define where the guide belonged, how it would complement component guidance, and what access and review process we needed to publish.

For the creative direction team, contributing to Everest also meant learning GitHub and a documentation platform. We learned the publishing workflow as we prepared the guide.

Outcomes

More visits, broader use

100+
Monthly visits in Everest, up from roughly 12 in SharePoint
First

Live

Publishing path for the broader standards team

The rise in visits suggests that publishing in the team’s daily workspace helped the guide reach its audience.

As the first creative direction content published in Everest, the guide established a route the broader standards team could follow.

An AI writing agent now uses the guide as a drafting reference. Integration into VS Code–based prototyping workflows is in development.

Sources and measurement

How outcomes were measured: Internal page analytics recorded roughly 12 monthly visits in SharePoint and more than 100 in Everest. These rounded counts compare different reporting periods. They do not establish an exact growth multiple or isolate the effects of the move from the editorial changes.
