Case Study

Making a competency framework usable

I set an editorial standard and used AI-assisted revision to rebuild Schwab’s digital retail competency framework. Executive leadership and HR approved it for the 2026 performance review cycle.

Organization

Charles Schwab

Scope

Digital retail, product, design, content, accessibility

Timeline

October 2025 – present

Role

Senior manager, UX content strategy

What I owned: Content assessment, editorial standards, the AI revision workflow, and reconciliation of stakeholder feedback.

The problem

Unclear expectations across roles and levels

In October 2025, I took on the revision of Schwab’s digital retail competency framework. It covered core and relational skills, plus functional competencies in product, design, content, and accessibility.

The draft contained run-on sentences, overlapping definitions, repeated expectations across levels, and incomplete functional areas. Feedback from directors through executive leadership was extensive and often contradictory. Managers and employees lacked clear criteria for evaluating performance.

I needed to preserve the expertise in years of stakeholder input while making each expectation clear enough to evaluate.

The strategy

Define what a useful competency statement does

I set three requirements: each statement had to describe observable behavior, use active language, and express one clear expectation. Those rules guided both revision and review.

The standard also helped resolve conflicting feedback. I could assess a proposed edit by whether it made performance easier to evaluate.

The process

Revise one competency cluster at a time

I divided the framework into competency clusters and used AI to rewrite and consolidate each section against the editorial standard.

I reviewed each pass for new ambiguities, meaningful distinctions between job levels, and consistency across disciplines. Consolidation could remove repetition while also erasing an important difference; those decisions required editorial judgment.

For previously undefined areas, I used the same process to draft new competencies and refine them through review.

An external McKinsey team reviewed the framework against industry benchmarks.

What's next

In Q1 2026, I began adapting the revision workflow for Microsoft Copilot, focusing on applying the editorial standard to stakeholder feedback.

The longer-term goal is to flag departures from the standard and support new disciplines as roles change.
